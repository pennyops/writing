# Coral Mesa Regional Transit Authority
## Request for Proposals CMRTA-2026-014: Mobile Fare Payment and Rider Account Platform

Issued: February 2, 2026. Proposals due: March 13, 2026, 2:00 p.m. Eastern. This is a fictional RFP written for practice use; the Authority, its staff and all vendors named are invented.

## 1. Introduction
The Coral Mesa Regional Transit Authority (the Authority) operates fixed-route bus service, a two-route passenger ferry and an on-demand shuttle across a three-county service area. The Authority seeks a vendor (the Contractor) to provide a hosted mobile fare payment and rider account platform. Each numbered paragraph in Sections 3 to 9 is one requirement. In those paragraphs, "shall" and "must" state mandatory requirements; "should" and "may" state desirable features that are scored but not required.

## 2. Background
The Authority today sells paper passes and a reloadable card that is near the end of its life. About 41,000 riders travel on a typical weekday. The Authority expects the new platform to replace paper passes within two years.

## 3. Rider-facing requirements

### 3.1 Rider accounts
The Authority wants each rider to have a single account usable across bus, ferry and on-demand shuttle service.
3.1.1 The platform shall allow a rider to create an account with an email address or a mobile number, without requiring a credit card at sign-up.
3.1.2 The Contractor shall let a rider hold more than one fare product (stored value, passes and transfers) in the same account at the same time.
3.1.3 The platform must support family accounts in which one payer manages up to six linked riders.
3.1.4 The platform shall permit a rider to close the account and receive any stored value balance as a refund within ten business days.
3.1.5 The Contractor should offer sign-in with an existing Apple or Google identity.

### 3.2 Fare products and pricing
Fares are set by the Authority Board and change at least once a year.
3.2.1 The platform shall let Authority staff create, change and retire fare products without vendor involvement or a software release.
3.2.2 The platform shall apply fare capping so that no rider pays more in a calendar day than the price of a day pass, and no more in a calendar month than the price of a monthly pass.
3.2.3 The Contractor shall support reduced fares for seniors, riders with disabilities and students, verified once and valid for a period the Authority sets.
3.2.4 The Contractor must calculate transfers between modes within a 90-minute window at no extra charge.
3.2.5 The Contractor may support promotional codes for special events.

### 3.3 Payment methods
Riders pay in many ways, and many riders do not have a bank account.
3.3.1 The Contractor shall accept Visa, Mastercard, American Express and Discover credit and debit cards.
3.3.2 The platform shall accept Apple Pay and Google Pay inside the mobile app.
3.3.3 The platform shall allow cash loads at a retail network of at least 150 locations within the service area.
3.3.4 The platform must never store full card numbers on Authority or vendor systems outside the payment processor's vault.
3.3.5 The platform should support autoload, so a rider's stored value is topped up automatically below a threshold the rider chooses.

### 3.4 Validation and inspection
Fares are validated at bus fareboxes, ferry gates and by roving inspectors.
3.4.1 The Contractor shall produce a rotating visual ticket in the app that changes at least every 10 seconds to deter screenshots.
3.4.2 The Contractor shall display a scannable code that existing Authority validators read within 500 milliseconds.
3.4.3 The platform shall let a rider show a valid ticket without a data connection for at least 72 hours after the last sync.
3.4.4 The Contractor must provide inspectors with a handheld app that verifies tickets offline and records each inspection with time and location.
3.4.5 The platform shall prevent the same ticket from being used by two riders at the same time.

### 3.5 Trip planning and real-time information
Riders expect trip planning and live arrivals in the same app they pay with.
3.5.1 The platform shall consume the Authority's GTFS and GTFS-Realtime feeds without manual conversion.
3.5.2 The platform shall show live arrival predictions for every stop served by Authority routes.
3.5.3 The platform should suggest the cheapest fare product for a planned trip.
3.5.4 The Contractor shall send service alerts to riders who saved an affected route, within two minutes of the alert being published.
3.5.5 The platform may show crowding information when the Authority provides it.

### 3.6 Accessibility
The Authority serves riders of every ability and language.
3.6.1 The platform shall conform to WCAG 2.2 Level AA in the mobile app and the web portal.
3.6.2 The platform shall work with VoiceOver on iOS and TalkBack on Android for every rider function.
3.6.3 The platform shall be available in English, Spanish, Haitian Creole and Vietnamese at launch.
3.6.4 The Contractor must provide an equivalent non-smartphone option, such as a reloadable card, at the same fares.
3.6.5 The platform should support dynamic text sizes up to 200 percent without loss of content.

## 4. Security and privacy

### 4.1 Security
The platform handles payments and personal data, and the Authority's security office reviews it.
4.1.1 The Contractor shall maintain PCI DSS v4.0 compliance and deliver the current Attestation of Compliance before go-live and every year after.
4.1.2 The Contractor shall undergo an independent penetration test at least once a year and fix critical findings within 15 days.
4.1.3 The platform shall encrypt rider data in transit with TLS 1.2 or later and at rest with AES-256.
4.1.4 The platform must require multi-factor authentication for every Authority and vendor administrator account.
4.1.5 The Contractor shall notify the Authority of any security incident affecting rider data within 24 hours of discovery.

### 4.2 Privacy and data ownership
Rider data belongs to the Authority.
4.2.1 The platform shall store all rider data in data centers located in the United States.
4.2.2 The platform shall not sell, rent or share rider data with third parties for marketing.
4.2.3 The platform shall delete trip history older than 24 months unless the rider opts in to keep it.
4.2.4 The Contractor must give the Authority a complete export of its data in an open format within 30 days of contract end.
4.2.5 The Contractor should publish a plain-language privacy notice for riders, reviewed by the Authority before launch.

## 5. Implementation and training

### 5.1 Implementation
The Authority wants riders using the platform within one fiscal year of award.
5.1.1 The Contractor shall deliver a pilot with at least 500 riders within 120 days of notice to proceed.
5.1.2 The Contractor shall reach full public launch within 240 days of notice to proceed.
5.1.3 The Contractor shall name a dedicated project manager who attends a weekly status meeting with Authority staff.
5.1.4 The Contractor must provide a detailed implementation schedule with milestones and dependencies as part of the proposal.
5.1.5 The Contractor should migrate existing stored-value balances from the Authority's current card system.

### 5.2 Training and documentation
5.2.1 The Contractor shall train at least 40 Authority staff, including customer service, finance and inspectors, before launch.
5.2.2 The Contractor shall provide administrator documentation that is kept current with every release.
5.2.3 The Contractor should provide train-the-trainer materials the Authority can reuse.

## 6. Support

### 6.1 Support and service levels
The platform is a critical public service.
6.1.1 The Contractor shall be available 99.9 percent of each calendar month, excluding maintenance announced at least five days ahead.
6.1.2 The Contractor shall answer rider support requests by phone and chat from 6:00 a.m. to 10:00 p.m. every day.
6.1.3 The Contractor shall respond to a severity 1 incident within 15 minutes and restore service within 4 hours.
6.1.4 The Contractor must credit the Authority 5 percent of the monthly fee for each full 0.1 percent below the availability target.
6.1.5 The Contractor should provide a public status page.

## 7. Reporting and finance

### 7.1 Reporting and finance
The Authority's finance office reconciles every dollar collected.
7.1.1 The Contractor shall provide daily settlement reports that reconcile sales, refunds and processor deposits to the cent.
7.1.2 The Contractor shall deposit fare revenue into the Authority's bank account within two business days of collection.
7.1.3 The Contractor shall provide ridership reports by route, stop, fare product and hour, exportable to CSV.
7.1.4 The Contractor must give the Authority read access to raw transaction data through an API.
7.1.5 The Contractor should support the National Transit Database reporting the Authority files each year.

## 9. Proposal instructions

### 9.1 Proposal format and submission
The requirements in this section apply to the proposal itself.
9.1.1 The proposal shall be submitted through the Authority's procurement portal by 2:00 p.m. Eastern on March 13, 2026.
9.1.2 The proposal shall not exceed 60 pages, excluding appendices and resumes.
9.1.3 The proposal shall include three references from transit agencies of similar size, with contact names.
9.1.4 The proposal must include a signed acknowledgement of every addendum issued.
9.1.5 The proposal shall present pricing only in the separate price form, not in the technical proposal.

## 10. Evaluation
Proposals will be scored on technical approach (40 points), experience and references (20 points), implementation plan (15 points) and price (25 points). The Authority may award to the proposal it finds most advantageous.

The Brightwater platform will prevent the same ticket from being used by two riders at the same time (B-3.4).

## B-3.5 Trip planning and real-time information
The Brightwater platform will consume the Authority's GTFS and GTFS-Realtime feeds without manual conversion (B-3.5).
The Brightwater platform will show live arrival predictions for every stop served by Authority routes (B-3.5).
The Brightwater platform will suggest the cheapest fare product for a planned trip (B-3.5).
Service alerts reach followers of a route within five minutes (B-3.5).

## B-3.6 Accessibility
The Brightwater platform will conform to WCAG 2.2 Level AA in the mobile app and the web portal (B-3.6).
The Brightwater platform will work with VoiceOver on iOS and TalkBack on Android for every rider function (B-3.6).
English, Spanish and Haitian Creole are available at launch; Vietnamese follows six months after launch (B-3.6).
Brightwater will provide an equivalent non-smartphone option, such as a reloadable card, at the same fares (B-3.6).
The Brightwater platform will support dynamic text sizes up to 200 percent without loss of content (B-3.6).

## B-4.1 Security

The Brightwater platform will encrypt rider data in transit with TLS 1.2 or later and at rest with AES-256 (B-4.1).
Brightwater will notify the Authority of any security incident affecting rider data within 24 hours of discovery (B-4.1).

## B-4.2 Privacy and data ownership

The Brightwater platform will delete trip history older than 24 months unless the rider opts in to keep it (B-4.2).
Brightwater will give the Authority a complete export of its data in an open format within 30 days of contract end (B-4.2).
